Introduction

Purpose of this document

This document explains how the accounting system of Shiply Now is built and how its user interface works. It is written for an outside developer who is building a similar system and wants to understand the design decisions, the data model, the rules the code enforces and the screens that sit on top of them. It is not a user manual: it describes how the parts fit together and why, with short excerpts of the real code.

Every statement in this document was checked against the source code of the monorepo. Where the code and an older design note differ, the code wins. Where a feature was still being finished when this document was written, the text says so in a marked note instead of describing it.

What Shiply Now is

Shiply Now is a 60 minute delivery service for merchants who sell on Shopify, run from small dark stores in Greater Cairo. A dark store is a small warehouse that is not open to the public. A merchant sends stock to a dark store before any customer orders. When a customer orders, the store only picks, packs and hands the parcel to a rider, so the delivery can reach the customer within the promised time.

The system is a standalone pilot. It covers the whole chain:

  • dark stores (and hubs, which are sorting facilities) with delivery zones built from areas of Greater Cairo and opening hours;
  • the merchant catalog, matched to Shopify variants by SKU;
  • stock per store and bin, changed only through an append only log of stock movements;
  • a Shopify app that registers one fulfillment location per merchant shop and dark store, receives fulfillment requests and reports stock and tracking;
  • the order queue of each store with service level timers, and the staff screens that receive, pick, pack and hand over parcels;
  • the rider screens that deliver, collect cash on delivery and take proof photos;
  • an admin desk for operations, inventory, merchants, customers, human resources and sales;
  • an accounting system: a double entry ledger in piastres, cash control, merchant payouts, supplier bills, taxes, payroll and reports.

Cash on delivery is common in Egypt, so a large part of the accounting is about cash that riders collect for merchants, how it reaches a store box or a bank, and how and when the merchant is paid back what is theirs after our fees.

Conventions

Money in piastres

Money is stored as integers in piastres (1 Egyptian pound, EGP, is 100 piastres). Never as a floating point number. Values are converted to EGP only for display.

Every money column in the database ends in _piastres (for example amount_piastres on ledger_lines) and is an integer. The ledger refuses any line whose amount is not a safe integer. Percentages are stored as basis points, where 10,000 basis points are 100%: a fee of 2.5% is stored as 250 and VAT of 14% as 1400. A share of an amount is computed in whole piastres, rounded half up, by one helper:

/** A share in basis points of an amount, rounded half up, in whole piastres. */
export function share(amount: number, basisPoints: number): number {
  return Math.floor((amount * basisPoints + 5_000) / 10_000);
}

Amounts typed by a person are converted from text to piastres without going through a float (parseEgp in apps/web/src/lib/money.ts, described in the UI chapter). Amounts are shown with two decimals and Latin digits, kept left to right inside Arabic text.

Display in EGP

The screens show EGP with two decimals, for example 1,249.50. A negative amount shows in the danger color. Exports to CSV and Excel write money as EGP numbers with two decimals so that a spreadsheet can add them up.

Cairo time

The business runs on Cairo time, and Egypt has summer time, so the offset from UTC is not fixed (two hours in winter, three in summer). The rules are:

  • Instants are stored in UTC (occurred_at, created_at).
  • A business day is a Cairo calendar day written as YYYY-MM-DD. The helpers cairoDay(instant) and cairoDayStart(day) in packages/shared/src/hours.ts convert between the two and honour summer time. A fixed +02:00 is never used.
  • In SQL, a Cairo day or month is computed by converting from UTC first: (occurred_at AT TIME ZONE 'UTC') AT TIME ZONE 'Africa/Cairo'.
  • Admin and accounting date filters take Cairo days. A money event dated on a past day is booked at noon Cairo time of that day.
  • Date boxes in the interface are typed and shown day first (dd/mm/yyyy), as in Egypt, and sent to the API as YYYY-MM-DD.

VAT

VAT is added on top of our fees. Its rate is one global setting, fee.vat_bp, in basis points. The setting's code default is 0; the owner set it to 1400 (14%) on 11 November 2026. The same rate applies to rider office bills when the office has a tax number. VAT is never stored per merchant. When a fee is charged, the merchant's balance goes down by the fee plus its VAT, the fee is booked as income and the VAT is booked as owed to the tax authority (account 2301). Fees and VAT are computed separately, each rounded to whole piastres.

Document numbers

Accounting documents carry gapless serial numbers with a prefix, padded to six digits: JV-000001 for a journal entry, PAY- for payouts, CH- for cash handovers, BILL-, INV-, CN-, DN-, PO-, CHQ-, PC-, RW-, BS-, EC-, BD- and others. Numbers are taken inside the transaction that creates the document, under a database lock, so there are no gaps and no duplicates.

Language and wording

The interface exists in English (left to right) and Arabic (right to left). Every string goes through translation files; none is written into a component. The code and its comments use plain words. This document follows the same habit, and uses the words of the code (for example "wallet" for an account that belongs to one merchant, rider, supplier or team member).

The latest accounting features

The last group of features added to Accounting is described in full where each belongs:

  • Merchant payouts carry a statement of account (SOA-) frozen when they are prepared and a proof of transfer (POT-, the bank slip with its amount and reference) uploaded before they are paid; Accounting ticks payout checks in between, one of them by a second person who will not pay. See Merchant money (chapter 13) and Controls and access.
  • Suppliers have their own list and document page, and purchase orders can buy assets: each unit received becomes a fixed asset depreciated from the month it arrived (chapter 12).
  • Receipts and documents are chosen inside every money form and linked to the record in the same transaction; every journal entry has its own page with a printable voucher (chapter 12).
  • The postal fee: the postal authority's share of our fees per shipment, our cost, paid monthly from Taxes (chapter 10).
  • Budgets are planned per quarter, a 12 month forecast works with named scenarios, and Coming up and due lists every known payment with phone reminders (chapter 15).
